Decision: The sessions that the architect or the runner starts are recorded as dispatch records on the subtree root item they were given, with the SHA-256 of the session's credential (kept until the dispatch ends), the measured usage and cost, and no document kind of their own, and the board holds no price table, because an item already has history, `changes`, retried writes and the project check, while a new document kind would change the store's reads and writes, the snapshot, the events and the page, and the price depends on each message's prompt length, which only the transcript shows ([Dispatch records](../docs/design.md#5-data-format)), instead of a new document kind for dispatches or a price table on the board.

Decision: A dispatch that ends because its work must wait for hours, for another item or for a time, is recorded with a result of its own, put down to wait, beside the four others, counted in the number of dispatches, their minutes and their cost and in none of the four results, nor in the rework share or the means of kickbacks and rounds, because the owner ruled in the format of the dispatch record (item dispatch-format) that putting work down to wait is not giving it up, and a session that ends without the work having a result must not stand among the results, while the dispatch that takes the work up again carries the real one ([Dispatch records](../docs/design.md#5-data-format)), instead of recording it as given up, as a flag beside the result, or not recording it.

Decision: The board keeps one approved tag vocabulary in its own configuration, which only the architect changes after the owner approves on a card, and every item carries at least one tag from it that applies to its project once the vocabulary has one for the project, because the vocabulary is small board-level configuration under the same single-writer rule as the project list and a rename must change the vocabulary and the items in one commit, which one commit per operation already gives ([Tags](../docs/design.md#5-data-format)), instead of a document of its own for the vocabulary.

Decision: A work item may carry a difficulty from 1 to 5 as a field of the item and not of a plan row, every change is logged with the old value, the new value and the reason, and whether an item can be started is defined once, because the unit that is dispatched to an agent is the item and changing the level changes who does the work ([Difficulty](../docs/design.md#5-data-format)), instead of a level written into the text of a plan row or a level table kept on the board.

Decision: A turn of the architect that the runner starts on an item to answer an engineer or to check a merge is recorded as a dispatch of the role architect on the item it is asked about, as a record only that is issued no credential, with one result of its own, turn ended, and no counts, and the summary lists the turns apart as it lists the sessions put down to wait (in the number of dispatches and the cost, with the turns that ended and their minutes, and in none of the four results, the rework share or the means), because a dispatch credential is the access of its role and the architect's is the architect's whole access, which every caller of the dispatch tool, the runner without a credential included, would hold, a turn answers one question and comes to no outcome of the work, so as a result it could only be made up and would thin the rework share the levels are judged by, and the printed total stays the money spent ([Dispatch records](../docs/design.md#5-data-format)), instead of a credential for the role, a place among the four results or a summary that leaves the turns out.

Rejected:
- A new document kind for dispatch records: rejected because it would change the store's reads and writes, the snapshot, the events and the page's snapshot.
- A price table on the board: rejected because the price depends on each message's prompt length, which only the transcript shows.
- Recording a dispatch put down to wait as given up: rejected because the tallies would count paused work as given up, which the owner ruled it is not.
- A flag beside the result for a dispatch put down to wait: rejected because every tally would read two fields to know whether a dispatch had a result, and a session without a result would still stand in the result column.
- Not recording a dispatch put down to wait: rejected because its minutes and cost were spent, and the cost by level would miss them.
- A separate document for the tag vocabulary: rejected because every rename or removal would also have to write that extra file in the same commit.
- Difficulty written into the text of a plan row: rejected because the unit dispatched to an agent is the item, not a row.
- A level table on the board: rejected because the rules map levels to agents, outside the board.
- Issuing a credential to the architect's turn: rejected because the credential would be the architect's whole access for any caller of the dispatch tool, and the architect's credential for the runner was refused for that reason (responsibility/identities).
- Counting an architect's turn among the four results: rejected because a turn answers one question, so every result would be made up, and the turns would join the denominator of the rework share and thin it.
- Leaving the architect's turns out of the summary whole: rejected because the printed total would no longer be the money spent.

Decision: The state the runner last reported for the work on an item is one field of the item, `runner`, replaced by each report, where the board sets the times from its own clock, `since` stays while the state, the party asked, the cards, the items waited for and the event waited for with its wake time stay the same, a report identical to the stored one writes no commit, and only the architect writes it over a credential, and the runner itself without one from its own session, and the field is historical detail that is never news to the owner, because the whole view reads the state of every open item at once and a report is the current state and not a history, so a log entry per report would grow the item without bound while a document kind of its own would change the store's reads and writes, the snapshot, the events and the page, and the runner is part of the process ([Runner reports](../docs/design.md#5-data-format)), instead of a log entry for each report or a document kind for the runner's state.

Rejected:
- A log entry for each report: rejected because a report at every turn start and end would grow the item without bound, and the whole view needs only the current state.
- A document kind for the runner's state: rejected because it would change the store's reads and writes, the snapshot, the events and the page for a field of one item.

Decision: A runner report in the state `wait` records work that waits for an event settled beforehand and needs no one (the runner restarting, the main line's next merge, a set time), with the event as its own required field `what` and an optional UTC wake time `until`, and such a wait is on time until an hour after `until` or, with none, for 24 hours, because the runner reported such a wait as an ask to the architect, so the lists called the work asking someone and marked it overdue after that ask's one hour, which for a wait of a day is 23 hours of amber with nothing to look at, and the amber then stops meaning stuck; the event is a field of the wait and not the note so that `since` moves when the wait changes, and the hour after `until` and the 24 hours are provisional like the other limits and reopen when the live board's waits show them wrong ([Runner reports](../docs/design.md#5-data-format)), instead of reporting the wait as an ask, putting the event in the note or giving a wait with no `until` no limit.

Rejected:
- Reporting such a wait as an ask to the architect: rejected because the lists then say the work is asking someone and mark it amber after an hour though nobody is asked and nothing is stuck.
- The event in the note: rejected because a changed event would leave `since` where it was, or `wait` would need a rule of its own for the note, which would also be required for one state only.
- No limit for a wait without `until`: rejected because a wait for something that never comes would show as healthy for ever, the false trust of the other kind.
