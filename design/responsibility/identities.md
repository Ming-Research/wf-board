Decision: The architect is the author `Architect` writing from one of two session names, the one named for the architect, for the conversation the owner talks to, and the one named for the architect's turns, for the turns the runner starts to answer an engineer, and the PM is the author `PM` writing from the session `PM`, and the three session names are reserved (a credential of another role under them and a dispatch for them are refused), because each author name is also a project whose own engineers write as that author, so the author name alone cannot tell a project's engineer from the process role, instead of the author name alone as the identity ([Project responsibility](../../docs/design.md#9-project-responsibility)).

Decision: The architect's rights and the pushes for the architect are separate tests: both session names have the rights, while the pushes, a call's receipt for the PM's stream and the exclusion of the architect's own writes from its timed wake go by the conversation's name alone, and over a credential the receipt reads the role and the session together, because a turn must write on the engineer's item with the architect's rights, and under the conversation's name it would be carried the conversation's pushes and mark them delivered ([The architect's turns](../../docs/design.md#the-wake-streams)), instead of the turns writing as the conversation, a role of its own for the turns, or the runner writing the answers itself.

Decision: Writes under the retired coordinator author are refused by the one path every agent write takes, with a message naming the PM for the process and the architect for the rules, while the coordinator project stays configured so that its items, glossary and history remain readable, instead of leaving the author name usable.

Rejected:
- The author name alone as the identity of the PM and the architect: rejected because the author names `Architect` and `PM` are also projects whose engineers write as those authors.
- A role of its own for the turns, with a narrower access table: rejected because a turn does the architect's whole work (answering, cards, pauses, new items, ratings, the architect's journal), so the narrower table is either the architect's or stops a turn the next time a part is added, and a credential guards against a wrong name, not against impersonation, so least privilege buys little.
- The turns writing as the conversation, with the pushes kept by another mechanism: rejected because the board would still need a name or a credential to tell the turns apart, which is the chosen decision with an extra layer, and a flag on each call cannot be set by the runner for calls the model makes.
- The runner writing the turns' answers with its own session: rejected because it would translate each of the architect's judgment writes into a format it understands, and it would need the architect's rights itself.
