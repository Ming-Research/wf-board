Decision: The last job of a full CI started by hand starts with the run and not after the other jobs, waits until every other job has ended (a look at the Actions API every 2 seconds while more than 4 jobs are open, every second after that), writes the status `full-ci` itself (success when every job succeeded or was skipped and the total is not red) and puts into the run's summary the seconds from the start of the run to the status, the time of each job and a table of the test shards that names a shard over 1.5 times the mean of the others, with the total yellow above 50 seconds and red above 60, and a red total fails the status and the job, because a lane that slows down must turn something red or the minute for a pull request's checks is lost unnoticed, two runs of one configuration differed by 13% so a typical 50 seconds leaves the line 20% above, and a job that needs the others started 2 to 3 seconds after the last of them and set itself up after that, so that the status came 6 to 10 seconds after the last check where a job that waited wrote it 1 to 2 seconds after (8 samples each) ([The time gate](../../docs/design.md#the-time-gate)), instead of limits on each job's own time alone, no gate, a gate that only reports, or a job that needs the others. No job of the full CI may use `needs`, because a job is listed in the run only once the jobs it needs have ended, and the job that waits would not wait for it. The jobs it waits for have a timeout of 10 minutes each, each step of the job itself one (5 minutes, the script's 15) and the job none, because GitHub cancels a job for its timeout without running another step, so a timeout on the job leaves the status pending, while a step that times out fails and the step after it, which runs when a step failed and the script wrote nothing, writes the failure; a job that hangs is cancelled before the script's step ends unless it started more than about 5 minutes late, since a job's timeout counts from when it starts to run, and then the script writes a failure naming the job, else the step after the script's writes one that does not. The browser tests are a workflow of their own and not in this run, so the total is the script tests' and a red that never clears cannot happen.

Rejected:
- Limits on each job's own time alone: rejected because a lane can slow down by waiting for a runner with every job inside its limit.
- A table of times with no limit: rejected because nothing turns red when the run slows down, which was the state before the gate.
- A job that needs the others, with a lean step: rejected because it still starts 2 to 3 seconds after the last check and the status came 5 to 6 seconds after it, against 1 to 2 for the job that waits.
- The last check job writing the status: rejected because two jobs that end at the same moment each find the other still running, nobody writes, and the status stays pending.
- A timeout on the job that writes the status: rejected because a job cancelled for its timeout runs no step after it, so neither the script nor the step that writes a failure would run and the status would stay pending.
