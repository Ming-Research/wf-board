Decision: A request that reads the git history costs what the range it asks about costs, never what the length of the history costs, and runs under a budget held in its async context (bytes of history read, whole old boards rebuilt, and the heap staying under three quarters of its limit) that fails that request alone with an error saying how to narrow it, a history read that runs under no budget fails, and the caches of old documents, diffs and the one old board are bounded by the heap they are estimated to hold, because `changes` rebuilt a whole old board for each deleted document (about three seconds and half a gigabyte each on the synthetic board of that study, whose long texts are single-line quoted scalars; 56 MB on one shaped like the live board, and 30 MB once documents are parsed flat) and a range with ten deleted notes ran the heap into its 4 GB limit and killed the process together with every session's connection and the owner's page ([Request budgets](../../docs/design.md#4-architecture)), instead of running history queries in a worker thread or a child process with a memory limit; an allocation that reads no history is not stopped by a budget, which is accepted because a budget costs a few counters and stops what actually grew.

Rejected:
- A worker thread with a memory limit: rejected because it needs the board inside the thread (85 to 550 milliseconds and 41 MB for each copy), a message protocol for the six readers of history, and a test that no reader stays on the main thread, which is the same test a budget needs.
- A child process: rejected because it costs more than a thread and isolates more than is needed.
