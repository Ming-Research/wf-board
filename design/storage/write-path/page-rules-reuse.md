Decision: The page builds the rules of the state after a live change from the rules before it when the change wrote only items, in no field a status reads, and came in the same minute: it keeps every derived value, drops the checks and stalled counts of the items the write can reach (the written items and those whose clock or pause spans moved, their owners, the items they wait on and that wait on them, and the items whose step in progress names one of them or an owner of it, each with its owners), and answers for the time of the first build in that minute; it derives everything from scratch for any other change, and also when a write stamped before that time widens the active time or a session's write moves it into or out of its six-hour window, because a log write derived the whole board again for 55 to 80 ms of the page's main thread and pushed the jump to the next unread change past its limit on slow hosted runners, while the reused rules equal a derivation from nothing after random writes on a board of the live board's size and cut a write's cost to 0.46 of it ([Write cost](../../../docs/design.md#4-architecture)), instead of a derivation from scratch at every write.

Decision: The page keeps each item's part of the filter counts and, after a live change, counts again only the items it reaches, and keeps its copy of an item while the item is the same object, because counting every item and copying every item at each write were the next largest costs a write left on the page ([The page](../../../docs/design.md#7-the-page)), instead of counting and copying the whole board at each write.

Rejected:
- A complete incremental derivation that records what each derived value read and recomputes only what a write invalidates: rejected because every derivation would be rewritten to record its reads and every build would keep a dependency graph, while it gains over keeping the previous values only on writes to plans and statuses, a few a minute on the live board.
- Gathering the writes of a stretch of time into one and handling a navigation before them: rejected because each write costs the same, other sessions' writes show up later, and the writes put off still fall within the press of the key.
- Deriving the rules in a worker thread: rejected because the page reads derived values synchronously in hundreds of places.
