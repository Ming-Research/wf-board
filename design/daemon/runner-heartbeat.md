Decision: The runner's presence is one call, `report_heartbeat`, that the runner makes at every round of its loop, whether or not a turn is running, and that the board keeps as the time of the last call by its own clock, in memory and in a local file that git does not track (written at most once a minute), and the board tells the architect once with `runner-stopped` when the later of that time and the daemon's start is more than 10 minutes old and a heartbeat has been kept at least once, the next heartbeat ending the silence, because the runner is the one resident program and its stopping stops every item with no record on the board changing, the daemon is restarted at every deploy so a time kept only in memory would lose a runner that stopped just before one, the runner could not reach a board that was stopped, and a board that is to run on another machine can neither read a process table nor reach the runner ([The runner's heartbeat](../../docs/design.md#the-runners-heartbeat)), instead of using the beats of `report_live`, keeping the time in memory only, or committing it to the board's data.

Decision: The limit of silence is 10 minutes, a provisional choice: the runner's loop was not measured, so the figure only has to be well above one round and well below the hour of the architect's patrol, and it reopens if the push comes while the runner is there, instead of a figure taken from the runner's loop.

Rejected:
- The beats of `report_live` as the heartbeat: rejected because they are sent only while a turn runs, so a runner whose items all wait on CI or on a person sends none and the board cannot tell it idle from stopped.
- The time kept in memory only: rejected because a runner that stopped in the 10 minutes before a deploy's restart would never be told, which is the case the push is for.
- The heartbeat committed to the board's data: rejected because each is a commit in the write queue, 1,440 a day at one a minute against the 1,158 writes the whole board made on its busiest measured day, and a time that a restart may lose at the cost of one interval needs no history.
- The board reading the runner's process, files or port: rejected because a board on another machine cannot reach them, and the owner required that the board can move.
