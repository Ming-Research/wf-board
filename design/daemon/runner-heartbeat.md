Decision: The runner's presence is one call, `report_heartbeat`, that the runner makes at every round of its loop, whether or not a turn is running, and that the board keeps as the time of the last call by its own clock, in memory and in a local file that git does not track (written at most once a minute), and the board tells the architect once with `runner-stopped` when the later of that time and the daemon's start is more than 10 minutes old and a heartbeat has been kept at least once, the next heartbeat ending the silence and the file keeping that the silence was told so that a restart does not tell it again, because the runner is the one resident program and its stopping stops every item with no record on the board changing, the daemon is restarted at every deploy so a time kept only in memory would lose a runner that stopped just before one, the runner could not reach a board that was stopped, and a board that is to run on another machine can neither read a process table nor reach the runner ([The runner's heartbeat](../../docs/design.md#the-runners-heartbeat)), instead of using the beats of `report_live`, keeping the time in memory only, or committing it to the board's data.

Decision: The limit of silence is 10 minutes, a provisional choice: the runner's loop was not measured, so the figure only has to be well above one round and well below the hour of the architect's patrol, and it reopens if the push comes while the runner is there, instead of a figure taken from the runner's loop.

Decision: The heartbeat may carry the runner's pools of slots (key, name, slots, taken, and how long the queue has had nothing to start for the pool), which the board keeps as the last report with its arrival time in the daemon's memory only, and a pool with a free slot that has had nothing to start for an hour, counted forward from the arrival, is told once to the owner through the route a blocking card takes, the mark clearing when the pool is full, the queue has something for it or a report does not name it, because the slots and the queue are known only to the runner, which already calls the board every round, so the slots and its being there cannot be reported apart, and slots once stood empty for more than six hours with the owner away from the page ([The runner's heartbeat](../../docs/design.md#the-runners-heartbeat)), instead of a write tool of its own, the board counting the slots from the candidates, or a line on the page with no notice.

Decision: The hour is a provisional choice, taken from those more than six hours and not measured, and reopens if an hour proves too often or too late, and the pools are unknown after a restart until the next heartbeat, so a pool still idle then is told again, and the notice reaches only the pages open with notification permission, as a blocking card does, because the report is kept out of the data and the local file, a report a minute old costing nothing and the unknown minute after a restart being rare, instead of keeping the report in a file to avoid the repeat.

Rejected:
- The beats of `report_live` as the heartbeat: rejected because they are sent only while a turn runs, so a runner whose items all wait on CI or on a person sends none and the board cannot tell it idle from stopped.
- The time kept in memory only: rejected because a runner that stopped in the 10 minutes before a deploy's restart would never be told, which is the case the push is for.
- The heartbeat committed to the board's data: rejected because each is a commit in the write queue, 1,440 a day at one a minute against the 1,158 writes the whole board made on its busiest measured day, and a time that a restart may lose at the cost of one interval needs no history.
- The board reading the runner's process, files or port: rejected because a board on another machine cannot reach them, and the owner required that the board can move.
- A write tool of its own for the pools: rejected because the heartbeat and the pools could arrive one without the other, and the runner would make another call every round for what the heartbeat carries.
- The board counting the free slots from the candidates and the engineers working: rejected because the slot count is in the runner's configuration, which the board does not have, and a candidate is not what the runner's start check lets start, so the two would disagree.
- A line on the page with no notice: rejected because slots stood empty for more than six hours with the owner away from the page, and a line nobody reads is no line.
- The pools in the board's data or the heartbeat's file: rejected because a report a minute old is no loss and the only time it is unknown is the minute after a restart, which does not repay a commit or a file to keep in step.
