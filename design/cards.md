Decision: A decision card is one line of question and options, each with a short name and a detail (what it does, its cost, its risks, why it is or is not recommended), at most one of them recommended, and a confidence (its reason and an optional score from 1 to 5), and it holds no background: the head of its item gives the reasoning and places the card with a line that holds only its reference, a convention of the head's text and not a stored field, because the owner decides where the head gives the reasoning, and the head already says where each question belongs, so a stored index could disagree with it ([Decision cards](../docs/design.md#decision-cards)), instead of a long background on the card with only names in the options.

Decision: A card is stored once, with its item, and only a card that names the work it blocks is also a row of that item's plan, written by the card tool in the same commit and cleared when the card stops blocking, while the plan tools refuse card rows, so that the work goes on without a card that blocks nothing, instead of a row in the plan of every item that waits on the card or in the plan for every card.

Decision: A card carries a kind chosen where it is written, one of design (a decision that goes into the design tree), inquiry (any other question the owner decides) or rule (a change to the rules), required to create a card, changeable while the card is open, and read as design on a card written before the field existed, which stays without one in storage, because a reader that must tell the kinds apart (the runner's merge check holds a design card not yet ruled and lets an inquiry card pass) read the question's wording before and held an inquiry card whose question had no category prefix, while a stored field says the same to every reader and is set by the writer who knows what the card asks, and reading a missing kind as design keeps what the check did to every old card ([Card kind](../docs/design.md#decision-cards)), instead of a category written in the question or a hold only for blocking cards.

Decision: The board flags an open work item whose head was last revised before the owner ruled one of its cards with the check 做法待更新 (`approach-stale`), which asks the item's session to rewrite the head's 做法 as the chosen design and clears when the head is next revised; the head's last revision is read from the item's creation time and the latest log entry that says the head changed, and a ruling is later only when it falls in a later second, because the owner reads how a thing is done from the item's head and not from the pull request's design document, the rule that the head must be current had no check and left an item whose design was approved with its 做法 still at its start (the example that raised the card), and the two times the check compares are already on the board ([The check 做法待更新](../docs/design.md#checks)), instead of copying the pull request's design document into the item, a stored time of the head's last revision or a comparison with the item's `updated`.

Decision: Sending the owner's choice on a card records the ruling on the card in the same commit, a decision made in chat is recorded by a separate tool, and nothing deletes a card, an open card being retired only by withdrawing it, because no session should have to rule a card afterwards and a pick must not sit sent but unrecorded, and a closed card keeps its plan row as resolved, instead of a session ruling the card after the owner's pick or a card that can be deleted.

Decision: The commit that sends the owner's choice on a card also writes the choice note `done`, with no reply, when the note says nothing but its option, and leaves it `sent` when it carries the owner's own words or when its card was no longer open, because the ruling is all such a pick asks of anyone, so a session that reads the unhandled notes would find nothing to do with it (the runner starts an engineer turn for each sent note of an item), while the owner's words still have to reach a reader ([Ruling](../docs/design.md#decision-cards)), instead of closing every choice note or leaving each one sent.

Rejected:
- A long background on the card with only names in the options: rejected because the owner decides where the head gives the reasoning and each option's cost belongs beside its name.
- A stored placement field or an index of cards: rejected because the head already places each card and an index could disagree with it.
- A separate decisions section on the page: rejected because a card outside its item is read without the text that asks it.
- A row in the plan of every item that waits on the card: rejected because a card of another item is a shared dependency of the item that owns it.
- A plan row for every card: rejected because a card that blocks nothing would hold an item's status for a question the work does not wait on.
- A session ruling the card after the owner's pick: rejected because a pick could sit sent but unrecorded.
- Deleting a card: rejected because withdrawing is the one way to retire a card and nothing deletes one, so an open card stays in the history.
- Closing every choice note when it is sent: rejected because the owner's words in a changed pick would sit in a closed note that nobody is told of.
- Leaving a plain pick's note sent and having each reader skip it: rejected because the runner, the unhandled-note reads and the page would each need the same test, and a reader that forgets it starts an engineer turn for a pick that needs nothing.
- A category written in the question: rejected because the board does not check it and a card without it is read as a design card, the failure that held an inquiry card, while the reader still depends on the wording of the question.
- A hold only for blocking cards: rejected because an unruled design card that blocks nothing would then merge, and the rule that no design card merges unruled would rest on the engineer remembering to make it block.
- A migration that writes a kind into every stored card: rejected because nothing but the question's wording tells an old inquiry card from a design card, so the migration would guess; the open old cards get their kind from the architect with write_card, and closed ones need none.
- Copying the pull request's design document into the item: rejected because the page would read a private repository with a credential, the document is a long English text for implementers that the owner would still have to read through, and it does not keep the 做法 current.
- A stored time of the head's last revision: rejected because the log entry written in the same commit as the head already says it, so a field would duplicate it and every old item would need a backfill.
- A comparison with the item's `updated`: rejected because any write moves it (a log line, a plan row, a rating), so one such write after the ruling would hide a head that follows no ruling.
- Narrowing the check to design cards: rejected because a ruling of any card can settle how the work is done, and the cost of a one-line ruling is a one-line rewrite of the head.
